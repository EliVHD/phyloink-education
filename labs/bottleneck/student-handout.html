Population recovery and a missing allele

Predict before running. Compare the size schedule with allele frequencies and exact counts. This synthetic experiment is one reproducible trial set, not a forecast for real populations.

Setup and assumptions

Generation 0: N = 100, 100 A and 100 a copies. Bottleneck N = 5 at target generations 10–19; one-step recovery to N = 100 at generation 20. Constant-size condition keeps N = 100. Horizon 40; 4 trials per condition (8 total), master seed 1.

Synthetic neutral diploid A/a locus; closed, random-mating population and non-overlapping generations. Each target generation resamples 2N_t gene copies with replacement using the previous generation’s A frequency. One temporary reduction is followed by one-step recovery to initial N. This is reproduction/gene-copy resampling, not an instantaneous mortality cull or a sample of surviving individuals. No mutation, migration, selection, sex structure, linked loci, individual genotypes or pedigrees. Missing alleles cannot return through recovery alone. N is a model input, not an empirical population-size estimate; locus loss/fixation is not species extinction. H = 2p(1−p) is gene diversity at this one biallelic locus, not measured heterozygous individuals or whole-genome diversity. Conditions use separate seeded streams; matching trial numbers are not the same individuals or guaranteed counterfactual pairs.
