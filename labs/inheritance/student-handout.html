PHYLOINK / ADAPTABLE WALKTHROUGH

One locus: genotype and phenotype

Name: ________________________   Date: ______________

Each parent has two allele copies at locus A and contributes one copy to each offspring. Predict before revealing the model's probabilities.

Learn > Inheritance lab. Choose one locus; parents Aa x Aa. Sample size 32, seed 1.

Diploid autosomal loci; two alleles per locus; equal segregation; random fertilization; complete dominance; independent assortment for two loci. No linkage, mutation, selection, sex linkage, penetrance variation, epistasis or environmental effect.

A_ means AA or Aa; B_ means BB or Bb. These abstract labels do not represent measured organisms or traits from a tree.

  1. Which gamete types can each parent contribute, and with what probability?

  2. Predict the chance of an aa offspring. Explain your reasoning, then compare the genotype and complete-dominance phenotype tables.

  3. Sample 32 offspring with seed 1. Record expected and sampled phenotype counts. Repeat with seed 2, then with 1,000 offspring. Why need the sample not match an expected ratio exactly?

Two loci: combine independent probabilities

Name: ________________________   Date: ______________

Assume the A and B loci assort independently. Trace one allele at each locus from each parent. This is a stated model assumption, not evidence about a supplied tree.

Learn > Inheritance lab. Choose two loci; parents AaBb x AaBb. Sample size 32, seed 1.

Diploid autosomal loci; two alleles per locus; equal segregation; random fertilization; complete dominance; independent assortment for two loci. No linkage, mutation, selection, sex linkage, penetrance variation, epistasis or environmental effect.

A_ means AA or Aa; B_ means BB or Bb. These abstract labels do not represent measured organisms or traits from a tree.

  1. List each parent's gamete types and probabilities. Predict the chance of an aa bb offspring before revealing the square.

  2. Choose a Punnett cell producing AaBb. Is that cell's probability the total probability of AaBb? Use the aggregated genotype table to explain.

  3. Compare expected and sampled phenotype counts at 32 and 1,000 offspring, changing the seed for another trial. What would prevent you from applying this model to two linked loci?
