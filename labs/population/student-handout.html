Genetic drift: chance and population size

Name: ________________________   Date: ______________

Begin with equal fitness weights. Predict before running the trials. Each diploid individual contributes two gene copies to the count, so initial p(A) = 20/40 = 0.5.

Learn > Population evolution. N = 20; initial A copies = 20; 40 generations; 4 trials; seed 1. Fitness AA/Aa/aa = 1/1/1.

One diploid autosomal A/a locus; closed constant-size population; random mating; non-overlapping generations. Expected random-mating genotype proportions are fitness-weighted before sampling 2N gene copies. Individual genotypes and pedigrees are not tracked.

No mutation, migration, linked loci, growth, changing environment, sex structure or zero-fitness extinction. The no-drift reference is not the mean of finite trials under selection. These are invented model populations, not organisms inferred from a tree.

  1. Predict A frequency after selection but before finite sampling in the next generation. Can a realized trial rise or fall even though all fitness weights are equal?

  2. Run the trials. For Trial 1, record A copies, a copies and p(A) at generations 0, 20 and 40. Has A become lost or fixed? Compare with the other numbered trials.

  3. Repeat at N = 200 with 200 initial A copies, then try seed 2. What does larger N change in this model? What cannot be concluded from only four paths?

Selection with drift: expectation and trials

Name: ________________________   Date: ______________

Keep random sampling, but change the expected contribution of the genotypes. Relative fitness weights are not survival percentages. The reference calculation omits finite-population drift.

Learn > Population evolution. N = 100; initial A copies = 100; 40 generations; 4 trials; seed 1. Fitness AA/Aa/aa = 1.2/1.1/1.

One diploid autosomal A/a locus; closed constant-size population; random mating; non-overlapping generations. Expected random-mating genotype proportions are fitness-weighted before sampling 2N gene copies. Individual genotypes and pedigrees are not tracked.

No mutation, migration, linked loci, growth, changing environment, sex structure or zero-fitness extinction. The no-drift reference is not the mean of finite trials under selection. These are invented model populations, not organisms inferred from a tree.

  1. Predict the next A frequency after selection, before finite sampling. Explain how the three genotype weights contribute. Does a weight of 1.2 mean 120% survival?

  2. Run four trials. Compare their frequencies with the no-drift reference at generations 20 and 40. Why is the reference neither a guaranteed path nor a finite-trial mean?

  3. Hold N, initial A copies and seed constant, set all three fitness weights to 1 and rerun. Then change the seed. Does a rise or fixation alone prove selection or adaptation? Name one mechanism excluded from this model.
