# Teacher reference: common ancestry and clades

The questions are examples to adapt. Read the source limitations and check the intended interpretation before teaching. The diagram displays a sample of taxa from one published maximum-likelihood hypothesis; it does not claim a complete or final mammal phylogeny.

## Checked answers

1. Human and chimpanzee meet at source node `n003716`. Gorilla is not descended from that node. Their most recent shared ancestor with gorilla is `n003715`, a different ancestral node. Relatedness follows these connections, regardless of label proximity.
2. The displayed descendants of `n003715` are Homo sapiens, Pan troglodytes and Gorilla gorilla. Omitting chimpanzee excludes a represented descendant of the chosen ancestor. Emphasize "within the represented sample": the full source contains eight tips below that node, so these three are not its complete source membership.
3. Mus musculus and Rattus norvegicus meet at `n001377`, excluding the other displayed mammals. That node has 326 tips in the full source. The human/chimpanzee node has six source tips. Inspect full descendant membership before claiming that a sampled pair is a complete source clade. The complete underlying Newick and omitted-membership records are included.

These statements are checked against source paths in verification.json. No divergence ages follow from the drawing. The source branch distances are not rendered as time; no dating analysis is performed.

## Discussion and adaptation

Ask learners to explain each answer by tracing branches. If they answer using label distance, move to the alternative-layout example. Avoid presenting one extant organism as the ancestor of another, or calling a lineage more advanced because of its position.

For an introduction, retain only four taxa and ask one common-ancestor question. For more advanced learners, compare the six displayed tips with the complete source and distinguish a represented clade from full source membership. Provide source IDs when learners need unambiguous answers.

Use the unlabeled SVG only with an appropriate word bank or numbering that you add yourself. Preserve the same branch artwork in teacher and student versions. Keep citations and source terms with adapted materials.
